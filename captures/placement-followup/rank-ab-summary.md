# Balancer rank A/B on nachos: connections-first, handlers-first, sum

Host: nachos (Linux, io_uring, 12 physical cores). One tree (commit 7c6844c,
build in `rank-ab-build.txt`), one zio package, one pinned binary for all
three ranks; the rank is a run flag (`--balance-rank`) and the ready line
names it on every start. One rotating session, host idle before every round.

Arms:

- **WS, WS2**: work_stealing, same binary and arguments (the A/A pair).
- **PLC**: pinned + `.local` + balancer, rank `connections_first` (the
  committed default: connections, then handlers).
- **PLH**: rank `handlers_first` (the pre-fix rule: handlers, then
  connections).
- **PLS**: rank `sum` (connections + handlers, then connections).

Every balanced arm ran `--placement-log`, so its cost is the same for all
three ranks and absent from WS.

Rows: `rank-ab-sse500.txt` (20 rounds), `rank-ab-mix-unpaced.txt`,
`rank-ab-mixm-unpaced.txt`, `rank-ab-mix-paced.txt` (10 rounds each, 8 and 12
executors), `rank-ab-mixm-paced.txt` (**7 of 10 rounds**: a game started on
nachos and the harness refused to measure; the rotation is therefore not
balanced for that run). Tables: `rank-ab-tables.md`, regenerated by
`rank-ab-tables.sh`.

## How to read it

- Ratio cells: median per-round ratio arm / WS, with the number of rounds
  (n). `*` = outside this session's WS2/WS min-max. Inside the range means
  "not resolved", not "equal".
- **Heavy saturation is counted per heavy connection**: a heavy connection
  is saturated in a round when its own p50 is above 200 us. A round counts
  as saturated when any heavy connection is. "partial" (a stream that opened
  and delivered nothing, or ended early, or stopped) is a separate count.
- **Complete-delivery ratios**: each mix ratio is also computed over only the
  pairs where both rows delivered every stream to the end, with its own n.
  In this session they equal the nominal ratios in every cell, because no
  round was partial except PLH's two collapse rounds (below), which also
  drop out of the nominal ratios for lack of latency samples.
- **Churn on heavy executors**: from the placement log joined with the
  client's per-connection ports. The share of all churn connections placed
  on an executor that holds a heavy connection; "uniform" is the share a
  blind spread would give.
- `mix` = heavy connections are executors/2; `mixm` = executors/4. `unpaced`
  churn is about 5000 short connections per second; `paced` is 20 ms per
  short connection per worker (about 200 per second).

## Unit gates (tests/balance.zig, Mac, Debug)

| rank | inline pile-up (100 inline conns behind 1 idle SSE handler) | heavy executor (1 conn / 250 handlers against 2 conns / 20 handlers) |
|---|---|---|
| connections_first | [50, 51] pass | picks the heavy executor: **FAIL** |
| handlers_first | [1, 100]: **FAIL** | pass |
| sum | [50, 51] pass | pass |

## Plain read

1. **Connections-first sends churn to the heavy executors, as Fable said.**
   With paced churn, 95-96% of short connections landed on an executor that
   holds a heavy connection (a blind spread gives 50%). With unpaced churn
   it is 35%, but those executors are already busy, and 24 of 40 heavy
   connections were saturated in 10 of 10 rounds at 8 executors. Handlers-
   first and sum put 0-2% of churn there and had no saturated heavy
   connection in that shape.
2. **Handlers-first and sum give the same mix results.** Every mix row has
   PLH and PLS within about 0.1 of each other, well inside what the rounds
   vary. The difference between them is the unit gate: handlers-first fails
   the inline pile-up (live, it halved one-shot throughput in
   `defects-live.txt`); sum passes both gates.
3. **SSE 500 does not separate the ranks**: all 0/20 knee, p99 0.58-0.64.
4. **Unpaced churn at 12 executors is overload for every arm** (heavy
   connections saturated in 10/10 rounds for WS, PLC and PLS). It says
   nothing about rank, except that PLH collapsed there twice (below).
5. **Stopped streams are gone in every arm**, including WS in the unpaced
   mixes, where the 0932727 re-bench had 59-203 per shape (WS 59 and 196,
   WS2 62 and 203). This session is
   the first with the `waitUntilCb` clock fix (adb3ab4). The direct
   before/after (the pre-fix WS binary against the fixed one in the same
   rounds) has not run yet: nachos was taken by the game. Until it runs,
   "the fix removed them" is an inference from two different sessions.
6. **PLH had 2 zero-event collapses in 10 rounds** (unpaced mix, 12
   executors): 1619 streams got headers, then no events; 6 executor threads
   used 36-40 s of CPU and the other 6 used 13-21 s, in a run that normally
   takes about 11 s, until the client's safety timeout. This is the same signature as the
   PL collapse in the first session (t-2655). No other arm collapsed in
   this session. With 10 rounds per cell, 0/10 for PLC and PLS is **not**
   evidence that they cannot collapse, and 2/10 for PLH is not proof that the
   rank causes it (the chance of 0/10 at a true 1-in-10 rate is about 35%).

## Recommendation: switch the default rank to `sum`

- It is the only rank that passes both unit gates.
- In every mix it matches handlers-first and beats connections-first
  wherever there is churn beside heavy connections (worst heavy p99 0.54
  against 0.73 at 8 executors unpaced; 0.38 against 0.51 at 12 paced; no
  saturated heavy connection where connections-first had 24 of 40).
- It costs nothing measurable against the other ranks in SSE 500 or CPU.

What it does not fix, stated so it is not read as solved:

- It is still a count. An idle SSE handler weighs as much as a busy one, so a
  connection with N idle handlers still pushes up to N inline connections to
  other executors before the scores meet. Fable's target, a rank by measured
  recent work per executor, is the real fix; `sum` is the best count.
- It does not address the collapse (t-2655). A pinned default still waits on
  that diagnosis, on the drain spin (t-2652), and on the Mac re-bench.

Rejected: keep connections-first (the churn rows above, and the unit gate);
go back to handlers-first (fails the pile-up gate; same mix results as sum,
so it buys nothing); a weighted sum with a tuned handler weight (no data to
tune it, and it is still a count).

The default is **not** switched in this branch: the committed default is
still `connections_first`, so `test-placement` and `ci` fail on the new
heavy-executor gate until someone accepts this recommendation. The switch is
one line (`rank: Rank = .sum` in `src/edge/balancer.zig`).

## Summary

1. Connections-first puts churn on the heavy executors (95-96% with paced churn against a 50% blind share) and saturated 24 of 40 heavy connections at 8 executors unpaced; it also fails the new heavy-executor gate.
2. Handlers-first and sum give the same mix results (within about 0.1 on every row), but handlers-first fails the inline pile-up gate.
3. Recommendation: make `sum` the default rank; it is the only rank that passes both gates and it is never worse than the others in any measured shape.
4. Stopped streams were 0 in every arm and shape in this session (the first with the clock fix); the same-session before/after run is still pending.
5. PLH collapsed to zero events in 2 of 10 rounds at 12 executors unpaced; no other arm did, but 10 rounds cannot show the others are safe.
6. The paced mixm run has 7 of 10 rounds (a game took nachos); the clock-fix before/after, the live pile-up check per rank and the pin gate wait for nachos to be idle.
